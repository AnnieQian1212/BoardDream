# The 3-minute demo video

The submission needs a demo video of at most three minutes that shows what was built during the hackathon (all of BoardDream). Judges score Impact (30%), Use of Meta AI tooling (25%), Technical depth (20%) and Demo (25%: "is the demo video genuinely cool to watch?"), and the finalists' videos are watched live.

**The story: game night with friends.** A group that plays every week runs out of games; BoardDream makes a new one from a sentence, tested a thousand times, and the friends play it together that night, at one table by a link, with Muse Spark Open in any empty seat and house rules asked for in words. The friendship is shown, not claimed: friends joining by a link, two windows dealt in, rivals talking. The models and the testing stay the spine, since they carry 45% of the score.

The video has two parts:

| Part | Time | Reference | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. BoardDream in motion | 0:00–0:56 | Figma, [Introducing Figma Motion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l3HuAYXyOTo) | A fast reel of the real app in close-ups: music, no voice |
| 2. A primer with the builder | 0:56–2:59 | Linear, [Introducing Linear Initiatives](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlDVDvzCmn0) | Aichen Qian on camera between the app's shots, the narration as subtitles |

**The look.** Every shot of the app fills the frame. The camera pushes in and whips across it in moves of about a second (never past 1.5×, and by transform, so nothing snaps to whole pixels), and Part 2 stays mostly on the whole page. Nothing is framed in a box or laid over the page but a small label naming what is at work (a Meta model, a feature) and, in Part 2, the subtitles. Between the shots, cards in warm near-black with gold type (the app's Fraunces and Inter), words rising into place. Every cut has a handover, varied but in one family: a blur-zoom, a soft slanted wipe, a push, an iris, a fade, and a gold light leak over a hard cut. The takes are recorded at 1.5× the pixels (2880 × 1620) and the film is rendered at 2560 × 1440 and 60 fps, every frame a PNG, at 24 Mbps.

## What the references do

**Figma Motion (68 s).** Music only, no narration; about 20 hard cuts, one every 3 seconds. A 3-second abstract brand open, the product in context, a title card over a collage of examples, then seven features of 5–10 seconds, each a close-up of the real interface on a solid color with an oversized cursor doing one thing; a closing line and the logo.

**Linear Initiatives (4:14).** A 3-second black title card, "a primer with Nan Yu, Head of Product", then one continuous screen recording with the presenter in a round webcam bubble in the corner, no hard cuts, slow zooms. Who I am and the feature in one sentence; a concrete example walked through as they use it; a second use, done live; why we built it; an invitation to try it; the logo.

## Part 2: a primer with the builder (0:56–2:59)

A title card, "How BoardDream works, a primer with Aichen Qian". Aichen is on camera three times (the opening, "why", the close): head and shoulders in a gold ring on the left, over the app out of focus, with the two or three words being said arriving on the right; until those takes are recorded a quiet stand-in holds the ring. The app's shots are the take that made **Basalt Kin** for real at high effort with complexity at its highest (its making sped up 17×, with a badge giving the real 6 min 32 s, and a round of the mochi game at its own speed), and a take that plays it on: Muse Spark Open in the other seats, a house rule made version two, the board painted, the invite link. "Why" ends on two flows drawn in DashMotion's manner, the old way going round its loop a week a lap and BoardDream running from one sentence to a game played tonight; then a card of the four models. ## How it is made

| Step | Where | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Footage | `video/record` (the `ui-demo` skill: discover, rehearse, record) | `takes.mjs` drives the installed Chrome with Playwright at 1920 × 1080 CSS pixels drawn at 1.5× (`--force-device-scale-factor`), the table at `quality=medium`: an oversized ink cursor that glides before every click, visible typing, a rehearsal that checks every selector first. Frames come from Chrome's screencast and are encoded at 30 fps; a take slows its frame rate while a game is made, plays a round of the mochi game about once a minute, and marks the moments the edit cuts on. Takes: `make` (makes a game for real; the one used is saved as `making`), `after --game=<id>` (plays it on: rivals, a house rule, the painting, the link), `friends` (two accounts in two windows), `rivals`, `table`, `cards`, `painted`. Every game counts as seen, so no first-game guide covers a table, and a rehearsal stops before anything that spends model calls. The test accounts, Aichen and Mira, are made in the local database by `accounts.ts`, so no sign-up mail is sent |
| Edit | `video/remotion` (the Remotion skill) | Part 1 (`Reel.tsx`) and Part 2 (`Primer.tsx`) as scenes with a handover at each cut (`timeline.tsx`); the footage, camera, type, labels, cards and transitions in `fx.tsx`; `npx remotion render DemoVideo` (2560 × 1440, 60 fps, `remotion.config.ts`) |
| The two flows | `video/remotion` (the DashMotion skill) | One geometry (`flowLayout.ts`) drawn twice: `flows-html.mts` writes it as a stand-alone DashMotion page that DashMotion's checker passes, and `Flows.tsx` draws it in the film, everything moving on the film's clock |
| Camera and voice | Aichen | Part 2's three camera takes (opening, why, close) and the narration over the app, recorded against the cut; the takes go in `public/camera` and their names in `CAMERA` in `Primer.tsx` |
| Music | Aichen | A licensed royalty-free track; the rules disqualify assets used without the rights to them |

The footage and renders stay out of git; any machine with the servers running makes them again.
